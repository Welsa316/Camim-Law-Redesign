# Content Strategy

Campos Immigration Law. Spanish is the primary language of this site, so the Spanish is written first and the English is written second, as its own text rather than as a translation.

---

## 1. Audiences, in priority order

| # | Audience | Language | Device | What they need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A Spanish-speaking person with an immigration problem, in or near Orlando | Spanish | Phone | Proof this is a real licensed attorney who speaks their language, and a way to reach him today |
| 2 | A relative researching on someone's behalf, often the more English-fluent one in the family | Either | Phone or desktop | Credentials, the list of matters handled, how consultations work, what it costs |
| 3 | An English-speaking spouse, employer or sponsor | English | Desktop | Clear process explanation and a booking path |
| 4 | An existing client | Spanish | Phone | Pay an invoice, find the office, call |
| 5 | A producer, journalist or referring attorney | Either | Desktop | Biography, media history, admissions |
| 6 | Search and answer engines | Both | — | Unambiguous entity facts in plain HTML and structured data |

## 2. The anxieties the copy has to meet

Drawn from the questions the existing asylum page already answers, from the competitor research, and from the notario problem Florida legislated against in 2025.

1. Am I talking to a real lawyer, or to a notario who will take my money and hurt my case?
2. Will he understand me, and will I understand him?
3. How much does this cost, and what happens if I cannot pay it all at once?
4. Is it too late? Did I miss a deadline?
5. If I ask, will it get me in trouble? Is what I say private?
6. Does he handle *my* kind of case?
7. How long will this take?
8. What do I do right now, tonight, if a family member has been detained?

Every page is checked against this list. If a page raises one of these and does not answer it, it is not finished.

## 3. Value proposition

**Primary, in Spanish first:**

> Inmigración es lo único que hacemos. Hablamos su idioma y le explicamos su caso hasta que lo entienda.

**English:**

> Immigration law is all we do. We explain your case in your language, until it makes sense.

Why this shape. "Lo único que hacemos" is a factual scope statement, which Rule 4-7.14's comment expressly permits ("a lawyer may indicate that the lawyer concentrates in, focuses on, or limits the lawyer's practice to particular areas"), and it does the work that "specialist" would do without the rule problem. The second clause is the firm's own existing voice: the current homepage already promises "nos aseguraremos de que comprenda lo que está sucediendo con su caso". It is kept because it is true and because it answers anxieties 1 and 2 at once.

**Supporting messages, in order:**

1. A licensed Florida attorney, named and visible. Not a notario, not a consultant.
2. He explains immigration law in Spanish every week, on camera, and on television.
3. The consultation has a defined price and a defined shape, published up front. **[CLIENT VERIFICATION REQUIRED]**
4. Orlando office, with clients elsewhere in the United States and abroad because immigration is federal.
5. You will know what happens next, and when.

## 5. Voice

**Is:** plain, direct, warm without performing warmth. Short sentences. Second person. Present tense. Concrete nouns. Says the hard thing plainly and then says what can be done.

**Is not:** reassuring for the sake of reassurance, dramatic about the stakes, or padded with the vocabulary the brief bans.

**Banned outright**, in both languages:

- "Navigating the complexities of immigration law" and every variant of *navegar por las complejidades*
- "Your trusted partner", "su socio de confianza"
- "We're with you every step of the way", "estamos con usted en cada paso del camino"
- "Tailored solutions", "soluciones personalizadas"
- "Committed to excellence", "comprometidos con la excelencia"
- "Whether you're X, Y, or Z"
- "American dream", "sueño americano" — the category cliché, on half of the best-regarded sites researched
- "Fight for you", "luchamos por usted" — and any imagery implying prohibited tactics
- Any superlative, guarantee, percentage or "specialist" claim, per the Florida rules

**Reading level.** Spanish at roughly a B1 to B2 level: a reader with a secondary education should get through any page without a dictionary. Legal terms are introduced with a plain-language gloss on first use, then used normally. Form numbers always appear beside the plain name, never alone.

**Example of the voice, service page opening:**

> **Peticiones familiares (I-130)**
>
> Si usted es ciudadano o residente permanente, puede pedir a ciertos familiares para que obtengan la residencia. Quién califica y cuánto demora depende de su estatus y del parentesco.
>
> Esta página explica quién puede pedir a quién, qué pasa después de presentar la petición, y en qué casos conviene hablar con un abogado antes de enviar nada.

Against the pattern to avoid:

> ~~Navigating the complexities of family-based immigration can be overwhelming. Our dedicated team is committed to providing tailored solutions for your unique situation, every step of the way.~~

## 6. Call-to-action hierarchy

One verb, used everywhere, which the best-in-class research identified as a mark of the calmer sites.

| Level | Spanish | English | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | **Agendar consulta** | **Book a consultation** | Header, end of every page |
| Urgent | **Llamar ahora** | **Call now** | Mobile bar, detention page |
| Secondary | **Escribir por WhatsApp** | **Message on WhatsApp** | Mobile bar, contact page **[CLIENT VERIFICATION REQUIRED]** |
| Tertiary | **Ver servicios** | **See services** | Home, article ends |
| Client | **Pagar** | **Pay** | Header, footer |

Never "Sign up", which is what the current Services page says above a link that 404s. Never "Submit". Never "Learn more" without saying about what.

## 8. Educational content and its boundary

Immigration explainers are the firm's strongest content asset and its biggest compliance exposure. The rule the site follows:

**Explain the law. Never assess the reader's case.**

- Permitted: what a form is, who a category generally covers, what the steps are, what the government currently says about timing.
- Not permitted: "you qualify", "you should file", "your case will be approved", or any interactive tool that returns an eligibility result.
- Every educational page carries a dated general-information notice in its own language, and a line saying that immigration law changes and that the reader should speak to a lawyer about their own facts.
- Articles carry a visible author and a last-reviewed date. That is good practice for readers and it is also what answer engines use to attribute expertise.

## 9. Internal linking

- Every matter page links to its group, to two or three sibling matters, to the relevant FAQs, and to the consultation page.
- Every article links to the matter it relates to.
- The attorney page links to the consultation page and to the services hub.
- The detention page is linked from the home page, the header, and the removal-defence matter page.
- No orphan pages. Anything not reachable from the navigation in two clicks does not belong in the sitemap.

## 10. Bilingual working rules

1. Spanish is drafted first for every page.
2. English is written from the Spanish meaning, not translated from its words.
3. Slugs are translated: `/servicios/peticiones-familiares/` and `/en/services/family-petitions/`.
4. Chrome strings are reviewed with the same care as body copy. The research turned up an otherwise excellent trilingual site rendering "Home" as "Hogar" and a competitor shipping "DEFENSA DE DESPEDIDA" for removal defence. Navigation, buttons, form labels, error messages, the cookie notice and the 404 all get read by a fluent speaker.
5. Every required legal disclosure exists in both languages, beside the text it modifies, per Rule 4-7.12(c).
6. Layouts are tested against the Spanish string, which is the longer one.
7. No machine translation anywhere, and no browser-translate widget.
